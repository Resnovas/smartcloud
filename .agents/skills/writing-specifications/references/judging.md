# Judging a specification before you send it

Read it back as the receiving agent, and answer these four honestly.

1. **Do I know what "done" looks like without asking?** If not, fix
   validation.
2. **Do I know where to work, and what conventions apply?** If not, fix
   context.
3. **Could I deliver something that technically matches this and is not what
   was wanted?** If yes, the goal is stated as steps, or the scope section is
   missing.
4. **Is there a decision hidden in here that is not mine to make?** Name it
   in escalation, or make the decision yourself before sending.

If any answer is unsatisfactory, the brief is not ready. Sending it anyway
costs more than the minutes it saves: the agent will produce something, and
the something will have to be judged, explained and often discarded.

## Where specifications live

On the work-tracking card's `notes` field, because that is what a dispatched
worker actually receives. The same five sections apply when delegating
conversationally to a report; the card is just the durable form.

Write the brief before the card is claimed. Once a worker has claimed it, the
card is effectively read-only and the brief can no longer be corrected in
place - see the `workboard` skill.
